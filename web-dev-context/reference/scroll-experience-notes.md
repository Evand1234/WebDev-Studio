## The core idea Evan likes

It is not a stack of separate sections. It behaves like one continuous hero that keeps unfolding. The viewport appears to stay full-screen the whole time (pinned stage), and scroll drives a change of scene inside it: new background, new 3D/photographic subject, new headline, new paragraph. The explanation arrives as the visuals change, so each scroll step is one idea, one image, one short headline and a two to four line paragraph. There is never a "section break" feel.

## Structure and order (summary)

1. Hero: "Corn. Revolutionized." (green, corn husks)
2. Genetics: "First, a solid foundation." (orange, DNA helix, ring CTA)
3. Breeding: "Our breeders dial it in further." (green/teal, constellation then seed tin)
4. Field: "We take it to the field." (warm golden blurred field, plant)
5. Testing: "Testing, testing and more testing." (aerial plot grid)
6. Landing: sunset and seed, sign-up links (sky, bokeh)
7. Footer link list on bright green, then diagonal wipe back to hero.

It is a 5-chapter narrative (foundation, precision, field, testing, outcome) with the colour story going green, orange, green/teal, gold, lime/olive, sunset, bright green. Each chapter has a distinct hue so you always know the scene changed, but all are saturated and nature-derived, so they feel related.

## Transitions between sections

- No visible scrolling of a long page. Each scene replaces the previous in place while the stage stays pinned.
- Observed transition devices, by evidence in the stills:
  - Subject swap with a hue flip (green hero to orange helix): colours change wholesale in one scroll step.
  - Object-to-object continuity: helix then nodes with lines then network, so one glowing graphic morphs into the next.
  - Particles as glue: the same fine dust/sparkle layer persists across several scenes and changes tint, which hides the cut.
  - Blur-to-sharp: the field plate is heavily defocused; the plant is crisp.
  - Scale/zoom: plant and aerial grid appear larger than their previous frame (push-in feel).
  - Text stagger/scramble: headlines resolve from ghost/outline/doubled glyphs into solid white.
  - Diagonal clip-path wipe for the loop back (footer to hero).
- The sequence is paced so text for a scene is not on screen at the very moment the image changes (scene 1 and scene 5 are text-free): image lands first, then text.

## Background treatment

- Every scene is a full-bleed image or rendered scene, never a flat colour: gradient washes (radial, off-centre) plus particles plus vignette.
- A dark vignette at bottom and edges keeps white type and UI legible.
- Persistent fine particle layer (tiny pale dots, 1 to 3 px, with a few larger blurred bokeh circles) across scenes 1 to 4 and 9 to 10, giving depth and a sense of continuity.
- Colour is used as the section signal, not a UI chrome.

## The "realistic" imagery

- Hero corn cluster: photoreal husks and cob, dramatic rim lighting, cut out and placed on a graded green gradient. It appears to rise/scale slightly and blur when scroll starts.
- DNA helix: not photographic, but rendered like a long-exposure light painting (glowing fibres), so it feels physical. Moves by rotating and drifting.
- Seed tin and sample tubes: photoreal still-life props that enter from the bottom edge.
- Corn plant: photoreal 3D or cut-out plant with real depth of field against a blurred field plate. Moves by scaling and shifting laterally.
- Aerial plots: a real-looking drone or rendered orthographic plate with texture.
- Sunset kernel: a single backlit kernel with sparkles, very macro; realistic through depth of field and rim light.
- The impression of realism comes from lighting (rim light, bloom), shallow depth of field, grain/scanline texture and particles in front of the subjects, not from a video background.

## Typography

- Headline: heavy, wide-ish condensed grotesque in all caps (a bold sporty/industrial face, similar in feel to Bebas/Barlow Condensed ExtraBold/Anton family, but with rounded-square counters and slightly squared curves). Weight about 800 to 900, tight tracking (about -0.01em), tight leading (about 0.95 to 1.0).
- Size: hero headline about 8 to 9 percent of screen width per cap height (one line across about 75 percent of screen width). Chapter headlines about 5 to 6 percent of screen width, two or three lines, left aligned, with ragged right.
- Colour: pure white (#FFFFFF) with a faint outer glow/shadow for legibility over bright imagery.
- Body: a clean, heavy-ish sans, very small (about 11 to 13 px at desktop), white at 80 to 90 percent, about 4 lines, narrow column (about 25 to 30 percent of viewport), left aligned, roughly 12 px under the headline.
- Labels (SCROLL TO DISCOVER, links, CTA captions): tiny uppercase with wide tracking.
- Footer link list: the same condensed caps, centred, about 3 to 4 percent of screen width tall, stacked with generous line spacing; small dot/arrow glyph at the right of each.
- Layout: hero is centred; chapters are left-aligned in the left third at mid-height, leaving the right two thirds for the visual. That alternation (centred hero, left-aligned chapters) is a good rhythm.

## Motion language

- What moves: subject scale, subject position (drift), subject rotation (helix), backdrop hue, particle layer (slow parallax, different speed to subject), text (stagger/scramble reveal), clip-path wipes.
- Easing feel: scroll-linked and smooth; subjects glide (a long ease-out feel with inertial smoothing) rather than snap. Backgrounds swap fast (within about 1 s of the clip), but things inside them settle slowly.
- Parallax: at least three layers (background gradient, particles/bokeh, subject, text) moving at different rates. The particles and the helix clearly move independently.
- Pinning: the stage is pinned; scroll is a timeline, not a page distance. That explains the "one long hero" feel.
- Reveal: text arrives from ghosted outline/doubled glyphs to solid; images arrive via replacement/scale rather than slide.
- Scale: push-in on the plant and aerial plate (camera dolly feel).
- Blur: depth-of-field blur on the field plate; motion blur on hero corn during transition; heavy bokeh in the outro.
- Masks: a diagonal clip-path wedge for the loop transition; probably masked reveals on text.
- Overall tempo: about 1 to 1.5 s per scene in the recording (scrolling fast); the real pace at human scroll is likely about 2 to 4 s per chapter.

## How explanation text is paced against the visuals

- One idea per scene: headline (3 to 6 words, declarative, often a hook: "First, a solid foundation.") plus 3 to 4 lines of copy.
- Image first, text second: scenes 1, 3 and 5 show the visual with no or ghosted text; the text lands as the image settles.
- Text sits left, visual on the right or behind, so words never fight the focal subject.
- Headline sentences chain into a story arc: foundation, refinement, field, testing, then call to action. The copy is conversational and rhythmic ("Testing, testing and more testing").
- Paragraph copy is deliberately small: it is the optional layer; the headline carries the message for a skimmer.

## Reusable principles for building something like this in code (never copying)

1. Build one pinned, full-viewport stage. Scroll is a timeline (GSAP ScrollTrigger scrub or CSS scroll-timeline), not a page position. Everything inside is absolutely positioned and animated against progress.
2. Give each chapter its own colour world. Define a small set of CSS custom properties per chapter and crossfade/morph them with scroll progress, so a hue change is a single scrubbed value rather than a hard cut.
3. Layer: background gradient, particle canvas (cheap 2D canvas or a few hundred absolutely positioned dots), hero subject (cut-out PNG/WebP or WebGL), foreground bokeh, text. Different speeds per layer create depth.
4. Use a handful of real, high-quality cut-out subjects rather than video. Rim light, soft shadow and a faint bloom (CSS drop-shadow and blur, or a canvas glow) sell realism. Match each subject's light direction to its background gradient.
5. Use one persistent connective element (here, the particle dust and glow nodes) that changes colour but never disappears, so the swap between chapters feels like the same world changing.
6. Order the choreography: new background in, subject in, headline in, paragraph in, with 100 to 250 ms offsets. Hold text back until the image has mostly settled.
7. Text reveal: split headline into lines or characters, reveal with a clip/mask plus a very brief blur or letter-spacing settle. Keep body copy to 3 to 4 short lines in a narrow column.
8. Alternate centred hero and left-aligned chapter layout; keep the right two thirds clear for the subject.
9. Use depth-of-field blur as a transition: blur(0 to 20px) with scale 1.0 to 1.15 when a subject leaves, and the reverse as the next arrives.
10. Close the loop with a clip-path (polygon) wipe that reveals the first scene again, so the experience feels endless.
11. Make the scroll feel inertial: smooth the scroll value (lerp about 0.08 to 0.12) before driving animations, and set ease to power2/power3 out for entering elements.
12. Accessibility and performance: respect prefers-reduced-motion by turning the timeline into stacked static chapters; keep subjects as optimised WebP/AVIF at 2x; use will-change sparingly; cap particles on mobile; ensure body copy has a scrim or gradient behind it, because the source's small copy over busy imagery is weak on contrast.
13. Keep the text count tiny. The power of the clip comes from short headlines and a lot of image.
